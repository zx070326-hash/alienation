Metamaterials and wave control

Four theoretical threads, still open. Each asks how a structure lets a wave pass, stop, or travel in only one direction. The figures are schematics. They are not measurements, and they are not finished devices.

In one sentence

An arranged structure can let a wave travel one way, or stop it in a layer. This chapter asks where that limit sits. It is not a finished device, and this page has no measured curve.

Why it matters, and what is hard

A chip is a poor place for a magnet, yet light usually travels both ways. Stacking, a property that is still changing, and the direction of heat all mix “this can be done” with “this cannot.” An unfinished calculation is not a proof that it cannot.

The four cells name the questions. They have no scale, and they are not measurements or published performance numbers.

The question

A metamaterial changes the path of a wave by arrangement. The notes cover four lines: magnet-free optical isolation on a chip; how far a laminate can push an effective elastic property; where a time-varying response slows; and whether a directional difference in thermal radiation can cover a wide band. That directional difference is also called nonreciprocal.

These are theory and calculation. On this public site they are not presented as a citable paper. The photothermal report linked from the projects page is a separate delivery, already marked paused.

Four threads

Magnet-free optical isolation, on a chip

Light usually travels both ways. Isolation asks one way to pass and the other to be stopped. A common laboratory route uses a magnet. A chip is a poor place for one, so the question is how far the contrast survives without a magneto-optic material, and what it costs in bandwidth, loss, or an extra drive. The bound itself is not written here.

Limits of elastic laminates

Layers of different stiffness can behave, together, like another elastic solid. The normal of the layers and the direction of the load are not the same arrow. The question is whether some effective properties stay out of reach of stacking alone. This is elasticity theory, not a sample on a bench.

Reaction bottlenecks in time-varying systems

If the material itself is still changing, the response cannot speed up at every frequency at once. A bottleneck is where the exchange between ports slows, or cannot be done under the stated assumptions. An unfinished calculation is not a proof that something is impossible.

Broadband nonreciprocal thermal radiation

A warm body emits electromagnetic waves of its own. Nonreciprocal means emission and absorption need not match. A mismatch that lives only in a very narrow band is of limited use. The question is how wide it can become, and what stops it. The reasons should come from electromagnetic theory and the accounting of heat, not from taking another paper’s conclusion as the answer.

Method

Write the claim so that it can be refused. Then keep two ledgers. One ledger is construction: a stated route really can do the thing. The other is a limit: under the stated assumptions, it cannot. Calculation is used to tell those claims apart. It does not replace a derivation.

Some checks are sealed until the claim is written, and opened only for the comparison. What is inside the seal, which structures were tried, and the numbers, stay off this page.

Where it is

All four threads are still open. Some routes have been set aside. Setting a route aside is not a final bound. This chapter has no experimental curve, and it does not present the notes as a published paper.

A separate photothermal-metamaterial report is already public and marked paused. That page says it is not ready to fabricate. It is not this chapter.

What this chapter is not

The figures are schematics, not a breakthrough, a prize, or a finished sample. Numbers, candidate structures, and sealed checks stay out of the chapter.
