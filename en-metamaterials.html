CHAPTER / IN PROGRESS

Metamaterials and wave control

Four theoretical notes, still being written. They ask one kind of question: how a wave in a made structure can be blocked, passed, or allowed in only one direction. This chapter gives the direction, not the numbers.

The question

A metamaterial is a structure someone arranges. The arrangement can send a wave along a path a uniform material would not. I am not trying to show another finished device. I want to know where those paths stop.

All four threads are still theory. Calculation is there to check an idea. There is no citable final paper, and no experiment of mine that closes them.

Four threads

Magnet-free optical isolation, on a chip

Light usually travels both ways. Isolation asks it to travel mostly one way. A common laboratory route uses a magnet. A chip is a poor place for a magnet, so the question is how far isolation can go without a magneto-optic material, and what it costs. The cost might be bandwidth, loss, or an extra drive. The actual bound does not belong on this page.

Limits of elastic laminates

Stack layers of different stiffness and the stack can behave like one new elastic material. The number of layers and the stacking can change. The question is whether some effective behaviors stay out of reach of laminates. This is a question in elasticity theory, not a sample on a bench.

Reaction bottlenecks in time-varying systems

If the material itself is still changing in time, the response cannot become arbitrarily fast, and it cannot obey every frequency at once. A bottleneck is the place where it slows down, or cannot be done. The notes keep “this can be built” separate from “this cannot.” An unfinished calculation is not a proof that something is impossible.

Broadband nonreciprocal thermal radiation

A warm object emits electromagnetic waves of its own. Nonreciprocal means emission and absorption need not match. If that mismatch lives only in a very narrow band, it is of limited use. The question is how wide the mismatch can be, and what stops it from getting wider. The reasons should come from electromagnetic theory and the basic accounting of heat, not from borrowing a paper’s conclusion as the answer.

Method

Write the claim so that it can be refused. Then keep two ledgers. One ledger is construction: a stated route really can do the thing. The other is a limit: under the stated assumptions, it cannot. Calculation is used to tell those claims apart. It does not replace a derivation.

Some checks are sealed until the claim is written, and opened only for the comparison. What is inside the seal, which structures were tried, and the numbers, stay off this page.

Where it is

All four threads are still open. The notes run through November 2026. They have been revised, and some routes have been set aside. Setting a route aside is not the same as a final proof. There is no public experiment, and no paper to cite.

A separate photothermal-metamaterial report is already public and marked paused. That page says it is not ready to fabricate. It is not this chapter.

What this chapter is not

It is not a breakthrough, a prize, a finished sample, or a number that has been established. The figure and the text only say which four questions the notes are on, and that they are still theory and calculation.
