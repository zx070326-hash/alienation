ABOUT / alienation

Hey, I’m alienation.

Student at Chu Kochen Honors College, Zhejiang University — an unapologetically AI-Native builder focused on AI4Sci and AutoResearch, pretty sure AGI is about to clock in.

Plenty of people use AI as a polite autocomplete for boilerplate code or emails. I’m way more interested in handing agents a lab coat: letting them pitch hypotheses, write simulations, hunt for counterexamples that break their own ideas, and turn research from late-night artisan craft into a self-improving loop.

That’s why my desk looks slightly chaotic on the surface — agentic drug discovery (ETALON) on one side, multi-agent physics bounds for metamaterials and materials grand challenges (DMGC) on the other. Underneath, it’s all the same bet: what happens when AI stops being an assistant and becomes a genuine scientist?

Why “alienation”?

In a philosophy textbook, “alienation” sounds moody and dramatic. My reasons for picking it are much simpler:

First, staying half a step outside the default groove. Hang around any field long enough and “that’s just how it’s done” turns invisible. Playing the outsider for five minutes — asking “wait, why?” — is usually where the fun breakthroughs hide.

Second, embracing the AGI shift head-on. Intelligence is no longer a human-only club, and our relationship with knowledge and discovery is getting rewritten either way. Instead of stressing about being “alienated” by AI, I’d rather jump to the front of the wave and see how much cool science we can unlock together.
