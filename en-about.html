ABOUT / alienation

Hello, I’m alienation.

Chu Kochen Honors College, Zhejiang University — AI-Native, focused on AI4Sci and AutoResearch, convinced that AGI is on its way.

Using AI as a polite autocomplete for code or prose is the least interesting part. What holds my attention is bringing agents inside the scientific loop — proposing hypotheses, running simulations, hunting for counterexamples, and turning research from isolated craft into a system that compounds.

On the surface the threads span quite a range: an agentic harness for computational drug design (ETALON) on one side, and multi-agent bounds for metamaterials and materials grand challenges (DMGC) on the other. Underneath, it is the same test — once AI stops being merely a tool, where does the frontier move?

Why “alienation”?

The word carries a slight chill in the dictionary. I keep it for two quiet reasons.

First, half a step of distance from inertia. Stay inside any field long enough and default habits turn invisible. Stepping outside for a moment to ask whether something really has to work this way is usually faster than pushing harder in the same groove.

Second, clear eyes in the AGI era. Intelligence is no longer exclusively a human metric, and our relationship with tools and science is being rewritten regardless. Rather than worrying about being “alienated” by that shift, I prefer standing at the front edge to watch where it actually leads.
