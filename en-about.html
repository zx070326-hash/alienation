ABOUT / alienation

Hello, I’m alienation.

Student at Chu Kochen Honors College, Zhejiang University — an AI-Native builder focused on AI4Sci and AutoResearch, convinced that AGI is on its way.

Rather than treating AI as a polite autocomplete for code or prose, I care about bringing agents directly into the scientific loop: proposing hypotheses, running calculations, hunting for counterexamples, and pushing toward physical or algorithmic bounds — turning research from isolated craft into a checkable, self-improving system.

Across agentic drug design (ETALON), materials grand challenges (DMGC), and wave-control physics in metamaterials, the surface domains look different, yet the core question stays the same: once AI becomes a first-class participant in science, how should we design verification, tools, and research workflows?

Why the name “alienation”?

In a dictionary, “alienation” sounds cold or distant. I chose it for two very down-to-earth reasons.

First, keeping half a step of distance from inertia. Stay inside any discipline long enough and default assumptions start to feel invisible. Stepping back like an outsider — asking “does it really have to work this way?” — is often where the real bottleneck shows up.

Second, honesty about the AGI era. As intelligence stops being exclusively human, our relationship with tools, knowledge, and discovery itself is being rewritten. Instead of worrying about being “alienated” by technology, I’d rather stand right at the front of the wave and see how far AI-Native research can take science.
