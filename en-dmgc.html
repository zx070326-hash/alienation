DMGC, the materials challenges

Discovered Materials has published a set of long-standing materials problems and written them so that an answer can be checked. This chapter covers three of them, and the way the notes check themselves. It does not report a score.

In one sentence

When two materials meet, heat and electricity need not cross the same way, and an accurate calculation is often slow. This chapter reads three problems the public list still marks Open. It does not report a score.

Why it matters, and what is hard

What happens at an interface often depends on how the materials were made, not on one number the material is born with. If fast and accurate cannot be had together, the problem stays open. A calculation that misses means that model did not hold.

The question

The public list calls these problems still unsolved, and posed so that an answer can be checked. The notes cover three: heat at an interface, electrical behavior left by a process history, and the speed and accuracy of an exchange-correlation functional. The list contains other problems. This chapter does not take them up.

Public problem listRules

Three problems

P05, heat at an interface

Where two solids meet, heat does not always cross. A device that wants to waste less energy has to understand that boundary. The public problem asks for a prediction, not another sentence saying that interfaces matter. My notes are calculation and reading. They are not a new measured thermal conductance.

P02, electricity left by the process

The electrical behavior of a semiconductor interface is not a number the material is born with. It depends on how the interface was grown, cooled, and annealed. The problem asks whether that later behavior can be predicted from the process history alone. A model that falls over when the process changes has not held.

P03, fast and accurate

In quantum chemistry, an accurate method is often a slow one. An exchange-correlation functional is the piece of density-functional theory that stands in for how electrons interact. The public problem asks whether reaction energies, transition states, and electron transfer can stay accurate at a much lower cost. If accurate and fast remain a choice, the problem is still open.

Method

The check has an order. Write the claim first. That is the preregistration. Seal it, so the wording cannot be edited while looking at the outcome. Only then evaluate. If the evaluation fails, revise the claim, or set it aside in so many words.

Some computed models have already failed to match and have been set aside. The ones still in play are not settled. Which model, and by how much, is not written here. That would turn this page into a results table that is not ready to be public.

Where it is

All three problems are still being revised. The work is theory, calculation, and reading of the public literature. It is not a new measurement, and it is not a solution that has been submitted and accepted.

A failed calculation and a failed experiment are different. A calculation that does not match means that model, or that setup, did not hold. It is not evidence that the material itself cannot work, and it is not an experimental confirmation.

What this chapter is not

It is not a prize, a breakthrough, or a winning interface or functional. The problems come from a public page. My part is only this: the notes on these three are mine. Numbers, sealed predictions, and the specific models stay out of the chapter.
