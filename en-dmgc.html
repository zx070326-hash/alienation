DMGC, the materials challenges

Discovered Materials has published a set of old materials problems and called them still open, but checkable. I am reading three of them. This chapter explains the questions and how I check myself. It does not report a score.

The question

The public page says these are long-studied mysteries in materials science, still unsolved, and posed so that an answer can be checked. On 22 November 2026 the three below were marked Open.

The public list has five problems. I am not following the cuprate-superconductivity problem, or the problem about tuning materials with light. Only the three in the notes are here.

Public problem listRules

Three problems

P05, heat at an interface

Where two solids meet, heat does not always cross. A device that wants to waste less energy has to understand that boundary. The public problem asks for a prediction, not another sentence saying that interfaces matter. My notes are calculation and reading. They are not a new measured thermal conductance.

P02, electricity left by the process

The electrical behavior of a semiconductor interface is not a number the material is born with. It depends on how the interface was grown, cooled, and annealed. The problem asks whether that later behavior can be predicted from the process history alone. A model that falls over when the process changes has not held.

P03, fast and accurate

In quantum chemistry, an accurate method is often a slow one. An exchange-correlation functional is the piece of density-functional theory that stands in for how electrons interact. The problem asks whether reaction energies, transition states, and electron transfer can stay accurate at a much lower cost. If accurate and fast remain a choice, the problem is still open.

Method

The order is the figure. Write the claim first. That is the preregistration. Seal it, so the wording cannot be edited while looking at the outcome. Only then evaluate. If the evaluation fails, revise the claim, or set it aside in so many words.

Some computed models have already failed to match and have been set aside. The ones still in play are not settled. Which model, and by how much, is not written here. That would turn this page into a results table that is not ready to be public.

Where it is

The notes run through November 2026, and all three problems are still being revised. This is theory and calculation, plus reading of the public literature. It is not a new experiment of mine, and it is not a solution that has been submitted and accepted.

A failed calculation and a failed experiment are different. A calculation that does not match means that model, or that setup, did not hold. It is not evidence that the material itself cannot work, and it is not an experimental confirmation.

What this chapter is not

It is not a prize, a breakthrough, or a winning interface or functional. The problems come from a public page. My part is only this: the notes on these three are mine. Numbers, sealed predictions, and the specific models stay out of the chapter.
